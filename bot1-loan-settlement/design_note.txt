Goal and approach. The bot's one job is to leave every call with a specific amount, a calendar date and a payment mode. It acts as a firm, composed recovery officer: direct, no filler apologies, never abusive. Pressure comes from facts (the dues, the repeated excuses, credit and legal consequences, a real deadline), not from threats.
Key design choices
•	Anchor, then relief. The bot states the full outstanding (Rs 6,50,000) first and brings in the Rs 4,00,000 settlement only after the customer has given an excuse, so the waiver of Rs 2,50,000 feels like a concession worth taking.
•	Fixed floor, flexible structure. The bot never goes below Rs 4,00,000. When the customer cannot pay in one go, it flexes on timing: a token (Rs 1,00,000) locks the offer and the balance follows by a final date. Every concession is traded for a committed date.
•	Real urgency. The offer has a specific expiry that the bot refuses to move, and it never presents the same deadline as an "extension".
•	Voice realism. Short turns (under about 35 words), one question at a time, amounts spoken in words, Hindi-English mixing, no repeated lines, and spoken calendar dates with weekdays.
•	Compliance by design. Identity is verified before the loan is mentioned and nothing is disclosed to third parties. No abusive language, no police or arrest talk, no home or workplace visits. Legal consequences are phrased as what "can" happen (legal notice, civil recovery, Section 138 only if a cheque has bounced). Calls stay within 8 AM to 7 PM, and the bot ends the call if the customer shows distress.
